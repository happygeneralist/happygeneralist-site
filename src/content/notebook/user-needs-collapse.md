## Working definition

User needs collapse happens when the distinction between a user need and another design, policy or delivery concept breaks down.

A user need is no longer treated as what a person must be able to accomplish, understand, decide, access, maintain or resolve. Instead, it is collapsed into a task, feature, content requirement, user story, service output, policy objective or organisational priority.

## Status of this term

This is a Happygeneralist working definition.

It is part of the theoretical underpinning of Civic Design Intelligence and should not be presented as a settled term of art from the wider design field.

The point of naming it is to protect the conceptual separation that user-centred and civic design work depends on.

## What user needs must not collapse into

A user need should not be treated as the same thing as:

- a task
- a feature
- a user story
- a service output
- a content page
- a policy aim
- a metric
- a civic need
- an organisational need
- a one-off preference

These things may be related, but they are not interchangeable.

## Why it matters

When user needs collapse into delivery artefacts, the work can still look user-centred while losing the discipline that made user needs useful in the first place.

The team may still have needs in the backlog, on a slide or in a service assessment, but those needs no longer protect the relationship between evidence, lived experience, decisions and outcomes.

## Relationship to Civic Design Intelligence

Civic Design Intelligence depends on keeping different knowledge objects distinct enough to reason with.

The system needs to preserve distinctions between:

- capability
- civic need
- user need
- evidence
- insight
- behaviour
- emotion
- pain point or burden
- value
- outcome
- metric
- service obligation
- redress trigger

Collapse makes these objects less useful because it removes the grammar needed to trace why a decision was made and what kind of value it was meant to protect.

## Relationship to drift and erasure

User needs collapse is a structural condition that can make drift and erasure more likely.

If the organisation cannot distinguish a need from a feature, task, policy aim or metric, it becomes easier for the need to drift away from evidence or disappear inside delivery language.
