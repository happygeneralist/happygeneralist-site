## Definition

Hollow innovation is the introduction of changes or “advancements” that signal progress but do not deliver meaningful improvements in human outcomes. It thrives on spectacle, novelty and surface transformation, while leaving the underlying problems unsolved or even worsened.

## How it works

### Signal vs. substance

Innovation is packaged and broadcast as progress, but the lived experience of people does not improve.

### Performance of change

Efforts are optimised for announcements, launches and optics, not sustained outcomes.

### Adaptation burden

People — users, citizens, workers — carry the hidden costs of relearning, adjusting or tolerating disruptions that add no real value.

### Opportunity cost

Attention and resources are drawn into symbolic upgrades instead of addressing structural or systemic needs.

## Where it shows up

### Technology

Glossy redesigns, AI hype cycles and “disruption” narratives that do not translate into everyday benefits.

### Governance and policy

Reforms announced with fanfare but poorly implemented, producing little change in lived realities.

### Corporate responsibility

Sustainability or equity initiatives celebrated in PR but disconnected from real accountability or repair.

### Education and work

Programs framed as innovative yet more concerned with optics — new tools, certificates and announcements — than with learning or empowerment.

## Why it is a problem for institutions and brands

### Erosion of trust

People learn to expect promises without delivery. Updates and reforms are met with cynicism.

### Credibility debt

Every hollow innovation drains the reservoir of goodwill, making future claims harder to believe.

### Short-term gains, long-term fragility

Institutions get headlines, but lose the deeper legitimacy needed for resilience.

## Why it is a problem for humans

### Disempowerment

People adapt endlessly to changes that do not help them, reinforcing power asymmetries.

### Lost mastery

Hollow changes undermine stability and the ability to build confidence in tools, systems or institutions.

### Innovation fatigue

The gap between signal and lived experience creates exhaustion. People tune out or disengage.

### Stalled progress

Real opportunities for better outcomes are sidelined in favour of symbolic gestures.

## The human side of innovation

True innovation dignifies. It makes life more navigable, more just, more empowering.

Hollow innovation does the opposite: it hollows out meaning while dressing up the surface. It mistakes theatre for progress, spectacle for service. Over time, this does not just erode trust in a product or policy. It hollows out innovation itself.
