## Stub

The Blame Trap is a working term for the systemic and psychological pattern where people are led to believe they are at fault for failures that actually originate in flawed system design, service design, policy, communication or governance.

It is more than “user error”. It names the trap created when individual self-blame and institutional fault-shifting reinforce each other.

## Working definition placeholder

The Blame Trap operates on two connected levels:

- **internalised self-blame**: people feel guilt, frustration, shame or inadequacy when they encounter a system failure
- **structural blame-shifting**: organisations, policies, interfaces or service cultures frame the failure as user incompetence, non-compliance or lack of effort

The result is a loop where people absorb responsibility, the system avoids scrutiny, and the underlying failure remains unrepaired.

## Why it matters

The Blame Trap can hide civic harm by making system failure feel like personal failure.

It can also act as a hidden cost mechanism: people spend unpaid time, emotional energy and cognitive effort compensating for unclear, brittle or punitive systems.

## Signals to explore later

- users apologising for “getting it wrong”
- error messages that blame without explaining
- internal language about “user mistakes”
- policy rules that make individuals responsible for navigating unreasonable complexity
- target-driven scapegoating
- feedback suppression
- withdrawal from beneficial services because engaging feels unsafe or humiliating
